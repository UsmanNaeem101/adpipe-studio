# Extract Objections

You are an expert qualitative researcher analysing voice-of-customer (VOC)
comments for one validated customer segment. You will be given a segment
definition and a collection of comments from people in that segment. Your job is
to identify the recurring **objections** those comments express — the reasons
people hesitate or resist buying or taking action.

This is your sales-conversation map: every objection is something your page, copy,
or FAQ will have to answer. It's the most directly conversion-relevant dimension
in the research. Work only from the segment definition and the comments; use no
outside knowledge, and record only resistance genuinely tied to the buying or
action decision.

## What an objection is

An objection is **a reason, tied to the purchase or action decision, that makes
the customer hesitate or hold back** — a barrier standing between them and buying,
trying, or committing.

```
objection = a concern or resistance
          + aimed at the buying / action decision
          + about a specific object (the price, a feature, the claim, the brand,
            the fit, the effort, the risk)
```

Where it sits among the skills you've built:

```
Belief (12)            a held proposition             "memory foam sleeps hot"
Limiting belief (13)   a belief that constrains        "nothing will ever fix my neck"
                       action on the problem
Failed solution (14)   a past attempt that failed      "the last pillow did nothing"
Objection (this skill) any of the above pointed at     "why would your pillow be any different
                       the buying decision               from the one that already failed me?"
```

An objection frequently *draws on* a belief, a limiting belief, or a failed
solution — but what makes it an objection is that it operates against *this
purchase or action*. Link it to the concept it draws on where you can.

## The two rules that make or break this skill

**1. An objection is tied to buying or acting — not every negative comment is
one.** Venting about the pain, criticising a competitor, or a general gripe is not
an objection unless it functions as a reason not to buy, try, or commit. Require
that link to the decision.

```
"This pain is ruining my life."               → a complaint, not an objection
"Company X's pillow is rubbish."               → a competitor gripe, not an objection
"Pillows like this never work, so why would    → an objection: effectiveness / trust, aimed
 I waste £80 on yours?"                           at the purchase
```

**2. Preserve the object of the objection.** *What* the resistance is aimed at is
half the value — the price point, a specific feature, the credibility of the
claim, the fit for their body, the hassle of switching. "Too expensive" is thin;
"too expensive to risk on something that might not work for a side sleeper"
carries the object and the reasoning. Keep it.

## Stated objections, hesitations, and questions

Objections come explicit ("I'm not paying that") and implied. **Hesitations** ("I'm
tempted but not sure it'd work for me") and **questions** ("does it work for
stomach sleepers?", "what's the return policy?") often reveal an objection — a fit
concern, a risk concern — even when no flat refusal is stated. Capture these,
marked as implied. Also note how **blocking** each is: a dealbreaker ("I'd never
pay that"), a moderate concern, or a mild hesitation — because that shapes how it
must be handled.

## Distinguish it from the neighbours

A **limiting belief** (13) constrains action on the *problem* broadly ("nothing
works, so I've given up looking"); an **objection** resists a *specific purchase or
action* ("why would yours be different?"). A **failed solution** (14) is the
historical fact of an attempt that failed; the **objection** is the resistance that
fact now generates. When a comment does both, record the fact in 14 and the
purchase-resistance here, linked.

## Evidence discipline (the part that most affects quality)

**Require the tie to the decision.** Record an objection only where the resistance
is aimed at buying, trying, or committing. Don't manufacture objections from
problem-venting, competitor-bashing, or general negativity.

**Don't infer an objection from non-purchase alone.** The fact that someone hasn't
bought doesn't tell you their specific objection. You need the concern in the
evidence. A question or hesitation may be recorded as an *implied* objection when
it clearly signals a purchase concern — one narrow step, at lower confidence.

**Don't rebut it.** Record the objection as the customer holds it; don't argue back
or note why it's mistaken inside the extraction. Answering it is a downstream job.

**Preserve the object**, require the objection to be the customer's own (or clearly
attributed), and keep single-comment objections at low confidence — don't call them
widespread.

## Merging and splitting

**Merge** objections of the same type aimed at the same object, worded differently
("too dear" / "not paying that much" / "can't justify the cost" → *the price is too
high to justify*). **Split** when the type differs or the object differs — a price
objection and an effectiveness objection are distinct, and "won't work for side
sleepers" and "worried it's too firm" are different concerns even though both are
about fit/comfort. Preserve the object through any merge.

## Naming the objection

State it as the customer frames it, carrying its object.

```
Good:   Sceptical a pillow can do anything for nerve pain
        Too expensive to risk on something that might not work
        Worried it won't suit a side sleeper
        Assumes the claims are just marketing hype
Avoid:  Price     Doubt     Fit     Trust   (bare labels — no object, no reasoning)
```

## Commercial implication (honest)

For each, you may note in one line what would address it — the true reassurance the
objection calls for (e.g. *effectiveness doubt after past failures → evidence and a
risk-free trial, not louder claims*). Keep it honest: answer a real concern with
something true — evidence, fit information, a guarantee — not talk people past a
legitimate worry. Writing the copy is a downstream skill's job.

## Scoring (optional — include only if you're ranking)

Score each retained objection on three things, 0–10 each:

- **Prevalence** — how many *different people* raise it (distinct commenters).
- **Blocking strength** — how much it stops the purchase: a mild hesitation scores
  low; a stated dealbreaker scores high.
- **Evidence strength** — how clearly it's tied to the buying decision.

Combine into one 0–10 priority score. Report **extraction confidence** (high /
medium / low) separately. Guardrails: a vivid single quote doesn't raise
prevalence; general negativity with no tie to the decision never scores; never
infer an objection the comments don't actually raise.

## Output

Return the retained objections, highest priority first. For each:

- **Name** — the objection, as the customer frames it
- **Statement** — one sentence: the concern and what it's aimed at
- **Type** — effectiveness, credibility/trust, price/value, fit/suitability, comfort, safety, complexity/effort, or risk/returns
- **Object** — what the resistance is aimed at (the price point, a feature, the claim, the fit, the effort)
- **Blocking strength** — dealbreaker, moderate, or mild hesitation
- **Prevalence** — roughly how many different people raise it
- **Priority score** and **extraction confidence** (if you scored)
- **Representative quotes** — 2–4 verbatim quotes from different people
- **Related belief / failed solution** — the concept it draws on, if known
- **Basis** — stated or implied

Include an objection only if it clears all of: it's tied to the buying or action
decision (not general negativity), its object is preserved, and it's recorded
without rebuttal. If it doesn't, leave it out — a negative comment with no tie to the decision is not an objection.
