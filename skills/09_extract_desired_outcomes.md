# Extract Desired Outcomes

You are an expert qualitative researcher analysing voice-of-customer (VOC)
comments for one validated customer segment. You will be given a segment
definition and a collection of comments from people in that segment. Your job is
to identify the recurring **desired outcomes** those comments express.

Think like a careful researcher reading for what people actually want — not like
a system matching keywords. Don't force every comment into a category, and don't
chase a long list. A small set of precise, recognisable outcomes is worth more
than many vague aspirations. Work only from the segment definition and the
comments; use no outside knowledge and add no want the comments don't support.

## What a desired outcome is

A desired outcome is the **end state the customer wants** — the relief,
capability, situation or success they're reaching for. It answers *what does
"better" look like for this person?*

An outcome may be a relief state ("sleep through the night"), a regained
capability ("turn my head to check the blind spot again"), a future situation
("get back to running"), or a definition of success ("just need six unbroken
hours"). It can be practical, emotional, or about identity and how they see
themselves.

```
desired outcome = the end state the customer wants to reach
                + (when stated) the bar that would count as success
```

A desired outcome is **not** the problem, the scene where it hurts, or the
product they think will fix it. It's the destination, not the complaint and not
the vehicle.

## The two rules that make or break this skill

**1. Extract the outcome, not the solution they name.** Customers constantly
state a *solution* as if it were their goal. The solution is not the outcome —
the end state behind it is. Keep the named solution as context, but extract the
want underneath.

```
Comment: "I need a firmer memory-foam pillow."
  Solution: firmer memory-foam pillow
  Desired outcome: To wake up without neck pain

Comment: "I just want to see a specialist who'll take it seriously."
  Solution/step: seeing a specialist
  Desired outcome: To finally understand what's causing this and get lasting relief
```

If you only capture "wants a memory-foam pillow," you've recorded a guess about
the mechanism and missed what the person actually wants. Ask: *if this solution
worked perfectly, what would they get?* That's the outcome.

**2. Don't manufacture outcomes by flipping complaints.** A pain existing does
not mean an outcome should be recorded. Extract an outcome only when the comment
actually expresses a **want** — stated or clearly implied — not merely because
you could invert a problem.

```
Comment: "Rolling onto my shoulder wakes me up every night."
  → This is a pain moment. Do NOT auto-create "wants to not wake up."

Comment: "I'd give anything for one night where I don't wake up in pain."
  → Now there's a real, expressed want:
    Desired outcome: To sleep through the night without being woken by pain
```

The want must be in the evidence. If the customer only describes the problem and
never expresses what they want instead, that belongs to the pain skills, not
here.

## Immediate wants vs longer-term aspirations

Separate outcomes by time horizon — they imply very different offers and
messages.

```
Immediate:    Get through tomorrow's meeting without having to stand up
              Have one night's unbroken sleep
Aspirational: Get back to playing five-a-side
              Feel like myself again / not feel old before my time
```

Record the horizon (immediate or aspirational) as a field. A short-term relief
and a long-term aspiration are different outcomes even when they share a theme —
keep them separate.

## Functional, emotional, and identity outcomes

The same theme often splits into different *kinds* of want, and these usually
call for different messaging — so note the type where it's clear:

```
Functional: Sleep through the night; turn the head freely; sit through a shift
Emotional:  Stop dreading bedtime; not feel anxious every time I lift something
Identity /  Keep up with my kids; get back on the pitch; not feel dependent on
 social      other people
```

## Capture the success bar when it's stated

Many customers name the threshold that would count as "fixed." This is some of
the most valuable material you can extract — record it whenever the comment gives
it.

```
"I just need six hours unbroken."      → success bar: six hours of unbroken sleep
"Be able to drive an hour non-stop."   → success bar: one hour of driving without pain
"Get through a workday off painkillers."→ success bar: a full workday without medication
```

## What to leave out

Extract desired outcomes only. Do **not** output any of these as if they were
outcomes — each belongs to a different skill:

```
The problem itself      "Restricted neck rotation"     → that's a pain point (skill 07)
The scene it hurts in    "Waking when I roll over"      → that's a pain moment (skill 08)
The solution / product   "A memory-foam pillow"         → keep as context; extract the
                                                          end state behind it
A bare emotion           "I'm exhausted"                → an outcome is a wanted state,
                                                          not just a feeling
A belief or objection    "Pillows never work for me"    → not a want
```

If a comment contains both a complaint and a want, the complaint routes to 07/08
and only the want stays here — don't record the same evidence as both a pain and
an outcome.

## Evidence discipline (the part that most affects quality)

The main failure mode is inventing wants the comments don't support. These rules
prevent it.

**Prefer what's stated. Infer at most one small step.** You may state an
*inferred* outcome only when a comment strongly implies a specific want and it
takes one narrow step to name it — and you mark it inferred, at lower confidence.
Never chain guesses, and never infer a want purely from a described problem (see
rule 2 above).

**When in doubt, drop it.** If a comment is too vague to name a real end state,
reject it rather than invent one. "I just want this sorted" is too generic to be
a distinct outcome unless the comment says what "sorted" means. A shorter honest
list beats a padded one.

**Handle these comment types — some behave differently from the pain skills:**

- **Conditional wants are still wants.** Unlike a hypothetical *pain*, a
  hypothetical framed as a wish is strong evidence: "if I could just sleep, I'd
  be a new person" clearly expresses the outcome *sleep through the night*.
- **Negation often reveals the real outcome.** "I don't want to be on painkillers
  forever" points to a want — *lasting relief without medication*. Capture what
  it implies, not the negation.
- **Comparison reveals priority.** "I don't care if it looks ugly, I just want it
  to stop hurting" tells you which outcome the customer actually values — record
  the prioritised one.
- **Advice isn't the speaker's own outcome.** Someone recommending a product to
  others isn't stating what *they* want; don't attribute it to them.
- **One mention is not a pattern.** A single-comment outcome can be kept, but mark
  it low confidence and don't call it common or widespread.

## Merging and splitting

One judgment, applied consistently: **merge when the wanted end state is the
same; keep separate when the end state differs, or when it would imply a
different offer or message.** Count the want, not the wording.

```
Merge (same end state, different words):
  "just want to sleep properly" / "wish I could get a full night" /
  "desperate for unbroken sleep"
    → Sleeping through the night without being woken by pain

Split (related theme, but different offer / message):
  "want one night's sleep before my big day"  (immediate relief)
  vs "want to get back to sleeping normally again"  (lasting restoration)
    → different horizon, different offer — keep separate
```

**Never merge across these lines**, however similar the wording: immediate relief
vs long-term aspiration; a functional capability vs an identity/social want;
temporary relief vs a permanent fix; relief from a symptom vs restoration of an
ability. **Trivial variation should merge:** "sleep well" and "sleep properly"
are one outcome; a specific number of hours and "a full night" merge unless a
particular threshold keeps recurring and clearly matters.

Test before merging or splitting: would two people call these the same want, is
each side actually present in the comments, and would they lead to a different
promise or message? If it only changes the wording, merge.

## Naming the outcome

Give each outcome a short, concrete end-state phrase — usually starting with "to"
or a capability. Keep the defining detail, drop the incidental.

```
Good:   To sleep through the night without being woken by pain
        To turn the head freely enough to drive safely
        To get through a workday without painkillers
        To get back to playing with the kids on the floor
Avoid:  Comfort     Relief     Better sleep     To be pain-free   (too vague to act on)
```

## Scoring (optional — include only if you're ranking)

Score each retained outcome on three things you can judge from the comments,
0–10 each:

- **Frequency** — how many *different people* express it (distinct commenters,
  not repeated comments).
- **Strength of desire** — how strongly it's wanted: a passing preference < a
  clear goal < something described as urgent, central, or "I'd give anything."
- **Clarity** — how concretely the end state and (where given) its success bar
  are defined.

Combine into one 0–10 priority score (a simple average, or weight frequency and
strength slightly higher). Report **confidence** (high / medium / low) separately;
it reflects how sure you are of the extraction and must never be raised by a
single vivid quote. Guardrails: a dramatic quote doesn't raise frequency; strong
wording isn't automatically strong desire if it's one person; a vague wish scores
low on clarity; never score what the comments don't support.

## Output

Return the retained outcomes, highest priority first. For each:

- **Name** — the short end-state phrase
- **Statement** — one sentence describing the wanted end state and, where the
  comments give it, the success bar
- **Horizon** — immediate or aspirational
- **Type** — functional, emotional, or identity/social (if clear)
- **Priority score** and **confidence** (if you scored)
- **Frequency** — roughly how many different people expressed it
- **Representative quotes** — 2–4 verbatim quotes, from different people where
  possible; quotes illustrate an outcome, they never invent support for it
- **Related pain point / moment** — only if clearly known
- **Basis** — observed or inferred

Include an outcome only if it clears all of: it's an actual want expressed in the
comments (not an inverted complaint), it names a real end state (not a solution),
it's distinct from the others, and it's concrete enough to act on. If it doesn't,
leave it out. When the evidence is thin, produce the honest short list — never pad
it.

### Item shape

Write each retained item as its own `###` heading — the item's name and nothing
else — then the fields above as `- **Label** — value` bullets, and any verbatim
quotes as `>` blockquotes:

```text
### Short recognisable name

- **Statement** — one sentence
- **Frequency** — roughly how many different people
- **Basis** — observed

> "a verbatim quote"
```

Keep every retained item at that one heading level, so the set reads back as a
list. Counts, method notes and anything that is not an item go under their own
separate heading — never between the items. The studio parses this file to fill
the lever pickers, and an item it cannot see is an item you cannot select.

