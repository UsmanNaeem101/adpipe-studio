# 28 · Write the segment report

**The last step.** One document per segment that someone could actually build a
campaign from.

Attach every extraction you ran for this segment, plus the segment file itself.

You're assembling, not re-analysing. Every claim here already exists in an
extraction output — if something isn't in one, it doesn't go in the report.

## GATE — no new claims. Run this as a check.

Before you write the summary, list every finding you're about to state and which
extraction it came from. Anything you can't source, cut. Report that count: claims
made, claims sourced. They should match.

This is the step where invented detail creeps in, because a report *wants* to read
smoothly and the truth is patchy. Let it be patchy.

## Structure

```
WHO THEY ARE
  the segment in three sentences · how many comments · how many conversations
  what makes them different from the neighbouring segments

WHAT THEY'RE LIVING WITH
  top pain points (07) with counts
  the moments those pains actually happen (08)
  how they feel about it, in their words (10)

WHAT THEY WANT
  desired outcomes (09), most-wanted first
  what they assume the fix is (15)

WHAT THEY'VE ALREADY TRIED
  failed solutions (14), with how each one failed
  products and brands in play (21, 22)

WHY THEY HAVEN'T BOUGHT
  the ranked barrier stack (27)
  objections (18) with the exact phrasing
  proof they need (20)

WHEN THEY BUY
  triggers (16) — the why-now
  criteria they judge on (17), dealbreakers first

HOW TO TALK TO THEM
  their words (25) and their vivid language (26)
  the ten quotes worth stealing (24)

WHAT THIS MEANS
  the three things the page must establish before anyone buys
  the angle you'd lead with, and why
  what you don't know yet
```

## Keep the counts visible

Every finding carries how many distinct people it came from. A report that reads
confidently without numbers is how a four-comment finding ends up driving a budget.

Where a segment is thin, say so at the top rather than writing around it.

## End with what's missing

The most useful section in a research report is the one that says what the research
couldn't answer. Under-evidenced dimensions, contradictions you couldn't resolve,
questions you'd need new data to settle. Write it plainly — it's what stops the
document being trusted more than it deserves.
