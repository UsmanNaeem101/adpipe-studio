# 20 · Desired proof

What they need to see before they'll believe you. Your evidence checklist.

## What one is

A **specific form of evidence** answering a **specific doubt**.

```
❌ "I want it under £60" (criterion → 17)     ✅ wants before/afters from people with
                                                  real nerve pain
❌ "should have a nice case" (feature wish)   ✅ won't trust reviews — wants a physio's
                                                  endorsement
❌ "proof" (no form, no doubt)                ✅ needs a money-back guarantee to take the
                                                  risk off
```

Two halves: the doubt, and the thing that would settle it. One without the other
isn't a proof demand.

## The forms that recur

```
someone like me      testimonial from a person with their specific situation
authority            physio, doctor, specialist endorsement
demonstration        video, before/after, a visible mechanism
guarantee            returns, trial period, money back
independent          reviews they believe, third-party testing
duration             "does it still work after six months?"
```

## The scepticism about proof itself is a finding

When people say reviews are fake or influencers are paid, that's not an objection to
your product — it's an objection to your *evidence*. Record it, because it decides
which assets are worth producing. A segment that distrusts reviews and wants a
practitioner's word needs a completely different page from one that reads Amazon.

## Give me

Each proof demand, the doubt it answers, how many people want it, and their exact
words. Then flag which are cheap to produce and which are expensive — that's the
build order.
