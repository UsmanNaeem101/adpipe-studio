# 10 · Emotional states

How they feel about it. Not clinical diagnosis, not your interpretation — what's
actually expressed.

## What one is

A feeling **attached to something specific**. A bare feeling word is not a finding.

```
❌ "my neck hurts" (pain, no feeling)   ✅ despair at waking in pain every morning
❌ frustration (bare label)             ✅ frustration at having to ask for help to dress
❌ "has anxiety disorder" (diagnosis)   ✅ anxiety about lifting in case it flares
❌ "wants to feel calm" (a wish → 09)   ✅ dread of bedtime knowing the pain will wake them
```

The object is the point. "Frustrated" tells you nothing; "frustrated at having to
ask for help to dress" is a scene, a feeling and a loss of dignity in six words.

## Don't diagnose

You're not a clinician. "This person is depressed" is not yours to say. "Describes
feeling hopeless about ever fixing it" is.

## Don't upgrade the emotion

If they say "annoying," don't record "devastating." Intensity is data. A segment
that says "annoying" needs different copy from one that says "ruining my life," and
flattening them loses the distinction that matters most.
