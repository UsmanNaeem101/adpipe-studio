# 15 · Assumed solutions

What they think the answer is. Their theory of the fix, right or wrong.

## What one is

The **shape of solution** they expect to work — often stated while they're still
looking.

```
❌ "foam pillow did nothing" (failed → 14)    ✅ a firmer, more supportive pillow
❌ "I use a foam pillow" (neutral mention)    ✅ seeing a specialist who'll take it seriously
❌ "ruled out surgery" (rejected)             ✅ something that keeps the spine aligned at night
❌ "a firmer one — but that won't work"       ✅ a firmer pillow (record as assumed;
                                                  the doubt is a separate objection)
```

## Why it matters

This is the frame your marketing sells into. Where their assumption matches what
you offer, you meet them where they already are. Where it doesn't, you've found the
reframe you have to win before anything else lands.

If the segment assumes the answer is "a firmer pillow" and you sell a softer
adaptive one, that gap *is* your positioning problem, stated in advance.

## Record what they assume, not what's true

You are not evaluating whether the assumed solution would work. Half of these will
be wrong. Record them anyway, with how many people hold each one.
