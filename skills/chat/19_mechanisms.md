# 19 · Mechanisms

The customer's causal theory. Why they think the problem happens, or why they
think something works or fails.

## What one is

A **because**. A causal chain, not a description.

```
❌ "my neck kills every morning" (a pain → 07)   ✅ low loft lets the head drop, so the neck
                                                     bends all night
❌ "this pillow's too soft" (description)        ✅ too soft → head sinks → neck loses support
                                                     → morning ache
```

If you can't find the "so" or the "because," it isn't a mechanism.

## Record folk theories exactly as held

```
❌ "corrects" the customer's nerve theory in the record
✅ record the folk mechanism as held; note separately whether it's validated
```

This is your most direct route into copy. When you can explain the problem using
the customer's own causal model and then position your product inside it, you're
speaking a language they already accept. Correcting them first costs you the sale.

## Log competing mechanisms separately

```
❌ merge "too high" + "too soft" because they're the same pain
✅ log both as competing mechanisms for the same pain
```

Two explanations for one problem means the market is divided on cause — which tells
you which explanation you'd have to win, or whether to sidestep the argument.

## Flag incomplete chains

```
❌ "flat pillows wreck my neck" scored as a full mechanism
✅ record it, flag the middle step as missing
```

Half a chain is still useful, but mark it, so you don't later mistake an assertion
for an explanation.
