# 03 · Find the audiences

Read the evidence and tell me which groups of people are actually in it. Discover
them from what's there — don't bring a list of expected segments with you.

Attach the deduplicated evidence. Work in batches, then merge your candidates across
batches at the end. Say what the batches were.

## What a segment is

A group of people whose situation would make you write a different ad. Different
words, different offer, different landing page — not just a different adjective.

## The test that decides everything

**A segment must be true of someone before the problem started, or independent of
it. Never a response to it.**

Side sleeping is true of you whether or not your shoulder hurts → segment.
Nobody takes ibuprofen for a shoulder they haven't hurt → not a segment.

Sort everything you find into one of four piles:

```
SEGMENT        upstream of the problem, or unrelated to it, and durable
               desk worker · swimmer · side sleeper · new mother · large bust
               · dentist · rotator cuff tear · pregnancy

ATTRIBUTE      a stance or behaviour that exists because of the problem
               cost-sensitive · brace user · takes painkillers · surgery-averse
               · sceptical · researches heavily

JOURNEY        where they are in diagnosis or treatment right now
               awaiting an MRI · six weeks post-op · deciding on surgery · in physio

SYMPTOM        what the problem does to them
               can't sleep on that side · can't reach overhead · pain dressing
```

Only the first pile becomes segments. The other three are real and worth keeping —
attributes and journey states become tags you can apply to people *inside* a
segment, and symptoms belong to the pain-point step. Keep all four lists. Don't
delete anything, just file it.

A diagnosis is a segment, not a symptom: `rotator cuff tear` is something a person
carries around; `can't sleep on that shoulder` is what it does to them on a Tuesday.

## Don't collapse everything into one umbrella

If you find swimmers, lifters and painters all with cuff injuries, that's four
things — the three specific ones and the general one. Keep them all. Most people
never mention a sport, and the general segment is where they belong.

## GATE — recurrence. Run this in code, not by eye.

A candidate needs **at least 2 separate comments** to exist at all. One comment is a
person, not an audience. Print how many candidates died here.

## For each candidate, tell me

Its pile. A plain-language name. Who these people are in one line. Why an advertiser
would talk to them differently. The comment IDs supporting it. The words they
actually use. And how confident you are — strong, probable, emerging or weak.

Don't validate yet, don't force every comment into a candidate, and don't worry
about overlap between candidates. Step 04 is the sceptic.
