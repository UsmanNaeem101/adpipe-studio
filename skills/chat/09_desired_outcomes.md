# 09 · Desired outcomes

The end state they want. Not the product — the life on the other side of it.

## What one is

```
❌ neck pain (the problem)        ✅ to turn the head freely enough to drive safely
❌ a firmer pillow (a solution)   ✅ to wake up without neck pain
❌ "better sleep" (too vague)     ✅ to sleep through the night without being woken
❌ "I'm exhausted" (a feeling)    ✅ to get through the day without an afternoon lie-down
```

The test: a desired outcome survives a change of product. If the answer changes
when you swap the solution, you've recorded a solution, not an outcome.

## Go one step past the obvious

"To be pain free" is true of everyone and useful to no one. Push for what pain-free
would let them *do* — the specific activity, role or identity they've lost.

```
weak    to be pain free
better  to sleep a full night without waking
best    to get through a shift without asking a colleague to lift for them
```

That last one is an ad.

## Watch for the modest ask

The strongest outcomes are often small and specific — not "cured" but "able to
sleep on my left side again." People who've had a problem for years lower their
ambitions, and the lowered ambition is more persuasive to write to than the
fantasy. Record what they actually ask for.
