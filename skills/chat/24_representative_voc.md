# 24 · Representative quotes

**This one is a synthesis step.** It reads the evidence file *and* your other
extraction outputs, then picks the quotes that best carry each theme.

Attach the segment evidence plus whatever extractions you've run.

## The one discipline

**Verbatim. Always.** The moment you polish a quote it stops being VOC and becomes
copywriting — and its whole value was that a real person said it that way.

Typos stay. Swearing stays. Bad grammar stays. Trim from the ends with an ellipsis
if it's too long; never rewrite the middle, never fix a word, never merge two
comments into one quote.

## What makes a quote worth keeping

```
specific       names a moment, an object, a number, a time of day
self-contained understandable without the thread around it
voiced         sounds like a person, not a review-site summary
one idea       carries a single theme cleanly
```

The best ones usually have an unexpected detail. "It's like someone's got a
screwdriver in the joint" beats "the pain is sharp" every time, and you cannot
write it yourself.

## Coverage over beauty

Pick quotes that cover the *range*, not just the most quotable ones. For each major
theme: two or three quotes from different people, and where a theme has a mild and
an extreme version, take one of each. A swipe file of only the dramatic quotes will
mislead you about the segment.

## Give me

Grouped by theme, each quote with its comment ID and one line of what it's doing.
Then a short "best of" — the ten you'd hand a copywriter first.
