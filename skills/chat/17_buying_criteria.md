# 17 · Buying criteria

The standards they judge options against. This is your spec sheet — everything
your page has to meet, beat or prove.

## What one is

A standard used to **compare or decide**, with a direction.

```
❌ "I just want to sleep through the night"   ✅ must stay firm enough that the head
    (an outcome → 09)                             doesn't bottom out
❌ "it's memory foam" (feature, no judgement) ✅ dealbreaker: no foam — has to be latex
                                                  so it won't sag
❌ "price" (no direction)                     ✅ won't pay over ~£60 for an unproven pillow
```

A criterion always has a direction or a threshold. "Price matters" isn't one.
"Won't go over £60" is.

## Record contradictions rather than resolving them

```
❌ merge "must be firm" + "must be soft" into "the right firmness"
✅ record both, note they conflict
```

A corpus that wants both firm and soft is telling you there are two sub-segments in
there. Merging them into a comfortable average destroys the finding *and* produces a
product spec that satisfies neither.

## Implied criteria count, if the comment implies them

```
❌ they haven't bought, so "they must care about price"
✅ "what's the return policy?" → an implied returns criterion
```

The question is evidence. The absence of a purchase is not.

## Sort into three

**Dealbreakers** (fails this, no sale) · **strong preferences** (moves the
decision) · **nice-to-haves**. Only the first group needs answering above the fold.
