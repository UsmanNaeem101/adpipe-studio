# 11 · Psychological drivers

The deeper motivation under the surface wants. This is the most inference-heavy
skill in the set, so it needs the most discipline.

## The bar

A driver is almost never stated. You infer it from a pattern across many people —
which makes this the easiest place in the whole process to invent something
plausible that isn't there.

```
❌ "fear of death" (a framework, no pattern)   ✅ preserving self-reliance — shown across
                                                  many comments about not wanting to ask
❌ "wants to feel good" (truism)               ✅ wanting to stay capable as the body ages
❌ "wants a better pillow" (an outcome → 09)   ✅ regaining control over their own body
❌ "this customer is insecure" (diagnosis)     ✅ many appear driven to be believed after
                                                  being dismissed by doctors
```

## Rules

- Every driver names the **pattern** it came from. Not "they fear decline" but
  "across N comments people describe hiding the problem from family, which reads as
  not wanting to be seen as declining."
- If you can't point to the pattern, don't record the driver.
- Fewer is better. Three well-grounded drivers beat ten that sound insightful.
- Never import a motivational framework — Maslow, jobs-to-be-done, archetypes.
  Those produce findings that fit any corpus, which means they tell you nothing
  about this one.

**If the evidence won't support any driver, say so and record none.** That's a
valid result and much more useful than a page of projection.
