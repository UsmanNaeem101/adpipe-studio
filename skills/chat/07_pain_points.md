# 07 · Pain points

The recurring problems this segment has. What hurts, what's blocked, what's lost.

## What one is

A **problem state** the customer is in — durable, not a moment. "Can't sleep on
that shoulder" is a pain point. "Woke at 3am last Tuesday" is a pain moment (08).

## The boundaries that matter

```
❌ a moment          "waking after rolling onto it"        → that's 08
❌ a wish            "I want to sleep through the night"   → that's 09
❌ a bare feeling    "I'm exhausted"                       → that's 10
❌ a belief          "memory foam sleeps hot"              → that's 12
❌ a failed fix      "the foam pillow did nothing"         → that's 14
❌ a product         "I use a Tempur"                      → that's 21
❌ a diagnosis       "I have a rotator cuff tear"          → that's a cause, not a pain
```

A diagnosis is what they have. A pain point is what it does to them.

## Generic labels are the main failure

The trap is a label so broad it tells you nothing. In a shoulder-pain corpus,
"shoulder pain" is not a finding — it's the topic.

```
❌ shoulder pain            ✅ can't lie on that side for more than an hour
❌ discomfort               ✅ arm goes numb holding the phone to the ear
❌ limited movement         ✅ can't fasten a bra behind the back
❌ sleep problems           ✅ wakes 3-4 times a night from the same shoulder
```

If you could write the label before reading the corpus, it's too generic. Split it
until each one names something specific enough that a customer would recognise
themselves in it.

## Don't over-merge

"Can't reach overhead" and "can't reach behind the back" are two different pains
with two different ad angles. Merge only genuine synonyms — different words for the
same experience — never two experiences that share a body part.
