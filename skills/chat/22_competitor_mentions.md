# 22 · Competitors

The named brands, products, clinics and practitioners people discuss — and who
switches to or from what.

Runs on the whole corpus or on one segment. Like 21, it doesn't need segmentation
first.

## What one is

A named provider plus how it's regarded. Two kinds:

```
direct      does the same job you do — a rival pillow, brace, supplement
substitute  solves the same problem differently — physio, surgery, chiropractor,
            "just living with it"
```

**Don't ignore substitutes.** Most segments aren't choosing between you and a rival
product; they're choosing between buying anything and going to a physio, or doing
nothing. Those are the real competition.

## Capture the switch

The most valuable thing here is movement between options, with the reason:

```
from → to → because
physio → home device → "couldn't keep taking afternoons off"
brand X → brand Y → "the first one went flat in a month"
```

Every switch is an acquisition route, and the "because" is the copy.

## Rules

- Named only. "Some other brand" isn't a competitor mention.
- Never add a competitor you know exists but nobody mentioned.
- Record sentiment as expressed, and keep the split: a brand loved by six people and
  hated by five is not "mixed", it's polarising, and that's a finding.
- Keep how people actually spell it.

## Give me

Each competitor, its type, distinct people, sentiment, what it's praised or blamed
for, and the switches in and out. Then the substitutes ranked separately — that's
your real competitive set.
