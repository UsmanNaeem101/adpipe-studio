# Canonical Execution Preset Library — Expanded Lever Schema

> Version 1.2 — 60 execution presets using one consistent lever system.

## Philosophy

These levers configure **how a static ad is executed**.

They do **not** decide the message itself (that's the PICC Card).

Instead they control the psychological, rhetorical and visual execution of that message.

# Recommended Value Types

Each execution preset should configure every lever using predefined values rather than free text.

Examples:

- Brain Target → Neocortex / Limbic / Reptilian
- Tone → Mentor / Investigative / No-Nonsense / Friendly
- Mechanism Depth → None / Light / Medium / Deep
- Evidence Density → Light / Medium / Heavy
- Visual Complexity → Minimal / Moderate / Busy
- Human Presence → None / Hands / Face / Full Body
- CTA Friction → Low / Medium / High
- Hype Level → None / Low / Medium / High

This execution system intentionally excludes:

- PICC Card variables
- Script sequencing
- Storytelling structure
- Video pacing
- Shot lists
- Editing
- Landing page frameworks

Those belong in separate systems.

This library exists solely to define **how a static advertisement should execute its message**, regardless of the message itself.

# 01. Awareness Builder

## Purpose

Introduce an unaware or problem-aware audience to a problem, mechanism, or category without triggering resistance.

The objective is **not to sell**.

The objective is to make the prospect think:

> "I've never thought about it like that."

This preset should increase awareness, create curiosity, and open the door for future persuasion.

---

## Targeting

- **Segment:** Broad segment
- **Avatar:** Typical customer
- **Awareness Level:** Unaware → Problem Aware
- **State Segment:** Everyday situation before problem recognition
- **Belief to Attack:** "Everything is normal."

---

## Persuasion

- **Brain Target:** Neocortex
- **Primary Emotion:** Curiosity
- **Communication Objective:** Awareness
- **Primary Angle:** Education First
- **Concept:** Hidden Problem / Hidden Opportunity
- **Proof vs Emotion Bias:** 70% Education / 30% Emotion
- **Mechanism Depth:** Light
- **Evidence Density:** Light
- **Objection Focus:** None
- **Trust Strategy:** Gentle Teaching

---

## Messaging

- **Tone:** Mentor
- **Reading Level:** Year 7–8
- **Lexicon:** Simple
- **Directness:** Medium
- **Curiosity Level:** High
- **Relatability:** Medium
- **Authority:** Medium
- **Hype Level:** None

---

## Proof

- **Proof Source Mix:** 20% Expert / 30% UGC / 50% Data
- **Comparison Target:** Status Quo
- **Proof Visibility:** Low
- **Claim Specificity:** Moderate

---

## Visual Direction

- **Visual Style:** Educational Infographic
- **Hero Image Type:** Problem Illustration
- **Focus Object:** Problem
- **Visual Complexity:** Low
- **Background Style:** Clean
- **Colour Psychology:** Calm Blues / Neutral Colours
- **Contrast:** Medium
- **Product Visibility:** None
- **Human Presence:** Optional
- **Facial Expression:** Curious
- **Eye Contact:** None
- **Text / Image Ratio:** 60 / 40
- **Information Density:** Medium
- **Graphic Style:** Clean Editorial

---

## Offer

- **Benefit Priority:** Awareness Before Benefits
- **CTA Friction:** Low
- **Price Framing:** None
- **Value Framing:** Educational Value
- **Risk Posture:** None
- **Urgency:** None

---

## Compliance

- **Safety Claims:** Conservative
- **Compliance Notes:** Avoid exaggerated problem statements. Claims should educate rather than diagnose, guarantee outcomes, or create unnecessary fear.

---

## Execution Philosophy

This preset should **never feel like an advert.**

It should feel like:

- "Interesting..."
- "I didn't know that."
- "That actually makes sense."
- "Maybe that's what's happening to me."
- "I should learn more."

The audience should feel like they have **discovered something**, not that they are being sold to.

---

## Ideal Use Cases

- Top-of-funnel awareness campaigns
- Introducing a new product category
- Creating market awareness
- Challenging assumptions without confrontation
- Soft education before mechanism-focused ads
- Building curiosity before proof-led campaigns

---

## Avoid

- Hard selling
- Heavy urgency
- Strong guarantees
- Excessive proof stacking
- Aggressive emotional language
- Complex mechanisms
- Overwhelming statistics
- Direct product pitching

The objective is education first, persuasion second.

- Fearmongering

**Make the audience emotionally re-experience the pain they desperately want to escape.**

Unlike **Pain Agitator**, which focuses on the emotional intensity of the pain, **Identity Mirror** focuses on making the prospect think:

The objective is not to convince.

The objective is to create identification.

The audience should feel understood before they feel persuaded.

- **Avatar:** One clearly defined persona
- **Awareness Level:** Problem Aware

## Execution Philosophy

The audience should feel as though someone has been watching their life.

The creative should describe experiences they rarely tell anyone else.

Examples include:

- Small daily frustrations.
- Habits they've developed to cope.
- Things they secretly worry about.
- Embarrassing moments.
- Thoughts they have but never say aloud.
- Tiny routines they've normalised.

The reaction should be:

> "How do they know that?"

The creative should feel less like advertising and more like someone describing the audience's life with uncomfortable accuracy.

Only after that identification is established should future creatives introduce mechanisms, proof or products.

---

## Ideal Use Cases

- Sleep problems

- Teaching
- Heavy proof
- Statistics
- Product demonstrations
- Feature lists
- Mechanism explanations
- Hard selling
- Overly broad messaging

If everyone can relate to the creative, **it's too generic**.

The objective is to make one specific audience feel:

**"This ad was made for people exactly like me."**

# 06. Hidden Cause

## Purpose

Reveal an overlooked root cause behind a familiar problem, making the audience realise they've been solving the wrong thing.

Unlike **Awareness Builder**, which introduces a problem, **Hidden Cause** introduces a **new explanation** for a problem the audience already recognises.

The objective is to make the prospect think:

> "Maybe that's why nothing I've tried has worked."

This preset shifts blame away from the customer and onto an unseen cause.

The product is positioned as the solution because it addresses the cause rather than the symptoms.

---

## Targeting

- **Segment:** Existing problem sufferers
- **Avatar:** Customer frustrated by repeated failures
- **Awareness Level:** Problem Aware
- **State Segment:** Has already tried one or more solutions without lasting success
- **Belief to Attack:** "I've already tried everything."

---

## Persuasion

- **Communication Objective:** Reframe Understanding
- **Primary Angle:** Hidden Root Cause

- **Mechanism Depth:** Medium

## Compliance

---

## Execution Philosophy

The audience should experience an **"Aha!" moment**.

The creative should challenge a commonly accepted explanation and replace it with a more compelling one.

Typical reactions should be:

- "Nobody has explained it like that before."
- "That actually makes sense."
- "Maybe I've been treating the symptom instead."
- "That's why nothing else worked."

The mechanism should be **simple enough to understand in a few seconds**, but interesting enough to make the audience want to learn more.

The creative should reward curiosity—not overwhelm with science.

---

## Ideal Use Cases

- Health and wellness
- Pain relief
- Sleep products
- Digestive health
- Skincare
- Hair restoration
- Ergonomic products
- Productivity tools
- Financial education
- Any product with a genuinely differentiated mechanism

---

## Avoid

- Overly technical explanations
- Dense scientific language
- Unsupported causal claims
- Feature-heavy messaging
- Emotional manipulation
- Long blocks of copy
- Making the customer feel responsible for the problem

The objective is simple:

**Replace the audience's existing explanation with a more compelling one that naturally makes your solution feel like the logical next step.**

# 07. Mechanism Educator

## Purpose

Teach the audience **how** the product works in a simple, memorable way that increases belief and perceived credibility.

Unlike **Hidden Cause**, which reveals *what* the real problem is, **Mechanism Educator** explains *why* the solution works.

> "That actually makes sense."

This preset transforms confusion into understanding.

When people understand the mechanism, the product feels less like a gamble and more like a logical decision.

- **Avatar:** Analytical customer who wants to understand before buying
- **Awareness Level:** Solution Aware → Product Aware
- **State Segment:** Comparing options and looking for a reason to believe

If not, it's too complicated.

The mechanism should remove uncertainty—not create more questions.

Complex science should always be translated into simple, intuitive concepts.
